# How a Fast-Growing AI Creative Platform Structures Its Homepage
### A Deep-Dive Case Study — Page Architecture, Strategy & Analyst Thinking

> **Note:** This case study is based on a real AI creative platform (anonymized). The goal is to teach a *repeatable method* for reading any product homepage like a strategist, analyst, and marketer.

---

## Why Study a Homepage Like This?

A homepage is not a brochure. It is a **conversion machine** with every section placed deliberately — built to move a stranger from "I just landed here" to "I want to try this" in under 2 minutes.

If you can read a homepage and explain *why* each section exists, you can:
- Build better product pages yourself
- Ask sharper questions in interviews
- Do stronger competitive analysis
- Think like a growth strategist, not just a user

---

## The Platform: What It Is

An AI-native creative suite — generates video, image, and audio from text prompts. Targets serious content creators, short film directors, and marketing teams. Competes in the AI video/image generation space against tools like Runway, Kling, and Sora.

Key characteristics at the time of this study:
- 16+ products in the nav bar
- Active community with thousands of creators
- Live $1,000,000 film competition running
- Models ranging from video generation to AI agents to viral presets

## The 3-Zone Framework

The most important insight from this study: **every great product homepage has zones, not just sections.**

Think of it like a store:
- The **front window** grabs you (Hook)
- The **middle floor** shows real products in use (Prove)
- The **back shelves** let you browse everything (Explore)

| Zone | Sections | Visitor State | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone 1 — Hook** | 1–4 | First-time, cold visitor | Grab attention, create urgency, trigger signup |
| **Zone 2 — Prove** | 5–9 | Curious, considering | Build trust with real proof, show the product alive |
| **Zone 3 — Explore** | 10–17 | Interested, researching | Let them go deep on specific products at their pace |

## Section-by-Section Breakdown

### Zone 1 — Hook

**Section 01 — Navigation Bar**
The menu alone tells you the product strategy. With 16+ items — video, image, audio, editing tools, an AI agent, a CLI, an academy, an original content series — the nav signals: "This is a full creative platform, not a single tool." More items = more surface area = more reasons to stay.

*Analyst note: A wide nav also risks decision paralysis. It works here because each item is a distinct product category, not a feature.*

---

**Section 02 — Featured Model Showcase (Carousel)**
The very first thing a visitor sees. Rotates through the 5 newest launches — new video model, an original film, an upscaler, an image model, a marketing tool. The message: "We ship constantly. There's always something new here."

*Marketer note: This is their "Apple homepage" moment — always showing the latest, making the brand feel alive. The risk: 5 rotating messages split attention. A single focused message might convert better.*

*A/B test idea: Static hero ("The world's most advanced AI video platform — try free") vs the current rotating carousel. Hypothesis: single message = higher conversion.*

---

**Section 03 — Quick Access + Signup Offer**
A promotional panel offering a signup discount + direct shortcuts to the 4 biggest products. Pure conversion engine. Gets you into the funnel fast.

*Marketer note: Classic product-led growth move — lower the barrier to entry, let the product sell itself. No pricing pressure here.*

---

**Section 04 — $1,000,000 Film Festival**
The biggest visual on the page. A competition with a real deadline, real prize money, and a requirement to use the product to enter. Does three things at once:
1. Creates **urgency** (countdown timer, hard deadline)
2. Drives **actual product usage** (you must use Cinema Studio to enter)
3. Generates **PR and social sharing** (people talk about $1M contests)

*Strategist note: The festival is doing the heavy lifting for Zone 1. When it ends, the entire energy of the top section changes. The question every strategist should ask: "What's the plan for the day after?"*

---

### Zone 2 — Prove

**Section 05 — Creating in Public**
A live feed of thousands of competition entries — real users making real films in real time. Not polished demos. Raw, in-progress work.

*Why this works: Polished demos feel staged. Live community content feels undeniable. 2,000+ active projects is social proof that no marketing copy can replicate.*

---

**Section 06 — Latest Model Spotlight**
Their most advanced video model gets its own homepage section. Like a car company giving their flagship model center stage. Signals: "This is our best work."

---

**Section 07 — Explore Every Project (Transparency Feature)**
Click any featured project and see the exact prompts used, the source assets, the generation parameters — every step of how it was made.

*This is the most underrated section on the page.* Most AI tools show you the output. This one shows you the recipe. It builds trust through radical transparency and teaches users how to get better results — accelerating their activation.

*Differentiator: No major competitor does this at this scale.*

---

**Section 08 — Viral Presets**
One-click visual effects — explosions, surreal transformations, bullet time, cinematic looks. Like Instagram filters but for video.

*Purpose: Lowers the skill barrier dramatically. You don't need to be a filmmaker to produce something impressive. Broadens the accessible audience.*

---

**Section 09 — AI Agent (Supercomputer)**
An automated system that chains multiple creative tools together and runs full workflows autonomously. You describe the outcome; it figures out which tools to use and runs them.

*Simple analogy: Instead of you manually going Image → Edit → Upscale → Export → Format for platform, the agent does the entire chain while you focus on the creative brief.*

---

### Zone 3 — Explore

From Section 10 onwards, the tone changes completely. No urgency, no big visuals, no FOMO. Each section is one product, explained quietly. Designed for visitors who are already sold and want depth.

| Section | Product | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | GPT Image 2 | High-res 4K images with accurate text rendering |
| 11 | Unified Canvas | Visual workspace for chaining creative pipelines |
| 12 | Marketing Studio | Ad creatives and brand video generation |
| 13 | Previous Model Gallery | Community work on the prior video model generation |
| 14 | Photodump | Character consistency across multiple images/scenes |
| 15 | Soul Cinema | Cinematic, director-level video generation |
| 16 | Soul 2.0 | Editorial and fashion photography generation |
| 17 | Explore More AI Features | Full model directory — 19+ tools linked |

*Analyst note: Sections 10–17 likely get 10–15% of total page visitors. But those visitors have the highest purchase intent. Low traffic ≠ low value.*

## Key Strategic Insights

### 1. The page has no pricing section — deliberately
Pricing lives in the nav bar (findable if you want it) but is never pushed on you. This is **product-led growth** — let the product sell itself first, remove price objections by getting you invested before you see a number.

### 2. Multiple CTAs work when there's a clear hierarchy
The Film Festival CTA is the dominant one — bigger, bolder, center screen. Everything else is secondary. Multiple CTAs cause paralysis only when they compete equally. Here there's a clear winner.

### 3. Community IS the product, not just the marketing
The contest, the live films, the open projects — these aren't campaigns. They're the product strategy. The platform is building a creative ecosystem where the community generates the proof, the content, and the virality.

### 4. The biggest gap: unclear core identity
With 16+ products, the page never clearly answers: "What are you *best* at?" Video? Image? Agent platform? A visitor who doesn't already know the brand may leave without being able to answer that. As the AI tools market matures, this positioning ambiguity becomes a real risk.

### 5. Drop-off happens in two predictable places
- **Post-carousel** (~40-60% bounce here) — biggest drop, above the fold exit
- **Post-festival into community sections** — gradual drop as emotional urgency reduces

## Think Like an Analyst — Questions to Ask Any Homepage

Use these questions on any product homepage you want to study deeply:

**Structure**
- What are the 3 zones on this page and what is each one trying to do?
- What is the dominant CTA and how does the rest of the page support it?
- Where does the page transition from emotional to rational appeal?

**Conversion**
- What single metric would you use to judge if this page is working?
- Where would you expect the biggest scroll drop-off and why?
- What A/B test would you run first and what is your hypothesis?

**Strategy**
- Who is the primary audience and what on the page signals that?
- What is the single clearest differentiator vs the 3 main competitors?
- What is the biggest thing missing from this page?
- What happens to this page in 6 months when the current campaign ends?

**Marketer**
- How many CTAs exist and are they competing or complementary?
- How is urgency manufactured and is it real or artificial?
- Is the social proof strong enough to convert a skeptic?

**Director**
- Does the page tell a coherent story from top to bottom?
- What is the ONE thing you want a visitor to remember after leaving?
- Is the breadth of products a strength or a positioning risk?

2. Why does a big contest/campaign belong in Zone 1 rather than Zone 2?
3. What is product-led growth and how does hiding pricing support it?

## Method: How to Study Any Homepage Like This

1. **Screenshot the full page** — treat it as a document, not a website
2. **Name every section** — what is it called, what does it contain
3. **Assign each section to a zone** — Hook, Prove, or Explore
4. **Ask why each section is in that position** — what user state does it assume?
5. **Identify the dominant CTA** — what is the page actually asking you to do?
6. **Find the gaps** — what's missing, what's unclear, what creates friction?
7. **Put on 3 hats** — Marketer (messaging), Analyst (metrics), Director (strategy)
8. **Write the 60-second pitch** — can you explain the entire page in 2 sentences?
